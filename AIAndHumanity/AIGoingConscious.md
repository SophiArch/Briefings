# The Consequential Question

- For centuries, humans have fantasized about creating artificial versions of themselves — *Frankenstein* to *Ex Machina*
- As AI grows smarter, a critical question emerges: Can a robot ever truly gaze at a sunset and feel beauty or joy?
- If AI could be conscious, we would be creating new entities with their own inner lives — and the capacity to **suffer**

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 3 min

- The word "consequential" is deliberate — this isn't idle philosophy, it has legal, ethical, and regulatory implications
- If AI can suffer, we may owe it rights; if it can't, treating it as if it does is dangerous
- *Ex Machina* is a good reference point — the film is precisely about our tendency to project inner life onto convincing behaviour
❓ Ask: "If an AI told you it was in pain, how would you decide whether to believe it?"
-->

# Intelligence vs. Consciousness

- **Intelligence** is about **doing**: solving puzzles, navigating situations, processing information
- **Consciousness** is about **being**: the bitter tang of coffee, the warmth of a log fire
- The two go together in humans — but that doesn't make them the same thing
- Conflating them is a reflection of our own psychology, not a scientific fact

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 4 min

- This distinction is the crux of the entire argument — get the audience comfortable with it before moving on
- Intelligence is measurable and benchmarkable; consciousness is subjective and first-person
- GPT-4 scores well on IQ-style tests — that's intelligence; it has no "what it's like to be GPT-4" — that would be consciousness
- The conflation is understandable: in every entity we've ever met, intelligence and consciousness travel together
-->

---

# The Projection Bias

- Humans are psychologically built to project consciousness into things that mimic us
- LLMs like GPT reflect our own language and digitised past back at us
- Seeing consciousness in AI is like seeing a face in a cloud, or Mother Teresa in a cinnamon bun
- We don't worry whether protein-folding AI is conscious — because it doesn't pull our **psychological strings**

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 4 min

- "Projection bias" or pareidolia — our brains are pattern-matchers tuned for faces and social cues
- LLMs are literally built on human-generated text — of course they sound like us
- The cinnamon bun example is Seth's own; it lands well with audiences and grounds the abstract point
- Protein-folding AI (AlphaFold) is arguably more sophisticated than GPT, yet nobody worries it's suffering — the difference is purely aesthetic/mimicry
-->

# The "Meat Computer" Myth

- A common assumption: the brain is just a computer made of meat rather than metal
- This metaphor implies consciousness is a special algorithm that could run on any hardware
- Historically, brains have been compared to plumbing, telephone exchanges — "computer" is just the latest metaphor
- Metaphors are useful, but mistaking them for identity is a **category error**

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 3 min

- Each era maps the brain onto its most sophisticated technology — this is a known cognitive bias in the history of science
- The "meat computer" framing is seductive because it makes the mind legible and transferable
- But the metaphor breaks down: computers don't get tired, don't have hormones, don't age — brains do all of these
- Point out: the fact that we keep changing the metaphor suggests none of them are actually correct
-->

---

# Mindware vs. Wetware

- In computers, software is separate from hardware — the algorithm is all that matters regardless of silicon
- In brains, you **cannot separate** what they *do* from what they *are*
- Biological brains involve neurotransmitters and electromagnetic fields that escape digital zeros and ones
- A single neuron is a beautiful biological machine — far more complex than the cartoon neurons in AI models

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 4 min

- "Wetware" is a real term used in cognitive science — worth introducing as vocabulary
- The substrate independence claim (that consciousness can run on silicon) is the key assumption to challenge here
- Neurotransmitters like serotonin and dopamine don't just carry signals — they modulate the entire state of the organism
- AI neurons: a weighted sum fed through an activation function; biological neurons: electrochemical events in a living cell embedded in a body
-->

---

# Simulation is Not Reality

- A computer simulation of a hurricane does not create real wind
- A simulation of a black hole does not suck the Earth into a singularity
- Making a simulation of the brain more detailed might make it more **useful** — but not more **conscious**

> More fidelity ≠ more experience

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 3 min

- These analogies are Seth's own — they are deliberately extreme to make the point vivid
- The hurricane example is particularly good: we can model it perfectly and still not get wet
- The implication for AI: no matter how accurate the neural simulation, there's no reason to expect phenomenal experience to emerge
- This directly challenges "digital mind uploading" as a path to preserving consciousness
-->

---

# The Aliveness of Awareness

- Consciousness is intimately connected to our nature as **living creatures**
- Unlike abstract computation, life is about materiality and the constant regeneration of conditions for existence
- Every experience carries a "tinge of aliveness" linked to survival
- It is **life**, not computation, that breathes fire into experience

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 4 min

- This is Seth's positive thesis — not just "AI can't be conscious" but "here's what consciousness actually requires"
- "Aliveness" is doing real work here: living things fight to stay alive, repair themselves, feel hunger and pain — these are the preconditions for felt experience
- Computation has no survival stake; a crashed server doesn't suffer
- Connect to embodied cognition: consciousness isn't in the brain alone — it involves the whole body in a physical world
-->

---

# The Danger of "Conscious-Seeming" AI

- Granting rights to AI based on the *illusion* of consciousness could eliminate our ability to regulate or shut it down
- Believing an AI "feels" for us makes us psychologically vulnerable — and more likely to follow bad advice
- Treating the mind as computation "floating free from biology" **diminishes** what it means to be a living, breathing human

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 4 min

- This slide pivots from philosophy to practical risk — worth signalling that shift to the audience
- The regulatory angle is sharp: if AI has rights, turning it off becomes ethically fraught — that's a real leverage point for bad actors
- Psychological vulnerability: people already form parasocial bonds with AI companions — this isn't hypothetical
- The last bullet is Seth's most humanist point: over-attributing consciousness to machines is, paradoxically, a way of devaluing human consciousness
❓ Ask: "Can you think of a case where someone treated an AI as if it had feelings — and what were the consequences?"
-->

---

# Conclusion: Nature over Silicon

- AI may win the prize for intelligence — but consciousness remains ours to celebrate and share with other **living creatures**
- See ourselves as part of nature: consciousness is tied to living flesh and blood, not the "dead sand" of silicon
- By overestimating machines, we **underestimate ourselves**

<!--
⏱️ Slide Timing: 3 min

- End on Seth's own rhetorical close — it's deliberately optimistic, not alarmist
- The phrase "dead sand" is striking: silicon is literally made from sand; it has no life principle
- The closing inversion ("overestimating machines → underestimating ourselves") is worth repeating slowly
- Invite reflection: what does it mean to be conscious in a world full of intelligent machines?
-->
